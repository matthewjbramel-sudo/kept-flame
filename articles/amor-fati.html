Amor Fati

Nietzsche called it his formula for greatness: amor fati — love of fate. Not resignation. Not "everything happens for a reason." Love. The demand is total: want nothing to be different, not forward, not backward, not in all eternity. Love every splinter of what happened, including the parts that broke you.

It sounds insane until you notice the alternative. Most of us run a background process called if only — if only I'd chosen differently, started earlier, been born elsewhere. It's the most expensive thought a mind can host. It rents space, pays nothing, and evicts the present.

What it isn't

Amor fati is not approval. Loving your fate doesn't mean the bad thing was good, or that you'd choose it again, or that justice doesn't matter. Nietzsche despised passive suffering — his whole philosophy is a war against the spirit that says "it is what it is" and lies down.

It's closer to what the Stoics called the dichotomy of control, sharpened to a point: some things are up to you, most aren't, and the ones that aren't are raw material. The question is never "why did this happen." The question is "what does a person like me make of this."

And it's kin to something older — the Christian-Buddhist thread that runs through this whole series. Ecclesiastes looked at the whole human project and called it hebel, vapor. The Buddha diagnosed clinging as the disease. Different vocabularies, same prescription: stop clutching at how things should have been, and work with how they are.

The practice

Pick one thing you're still arguing with — a failure, a loss, a wrong turn. Not the worst one. A medium one, to start.

Now run the experiment: for one week, treat it as necessary. Not good — necessary. As if the person you're becoming required exactly that fracture. Watch what changes. Usually what changes first is the energy bill: the if only process quiets, and the freed attention goes where it belongs — forward.

Nietzsche wrote that a man who has a why can bear any how. Amor fati is how you stop spending your why on the past and start spending it on what's next. Love your fate, and your fate starts working for you.
