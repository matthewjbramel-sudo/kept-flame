Chapter 9: After the Bloom

The city woke to green.

Kess stood on the Cradle's gantry — how they'd gotten up there, she wasn't entirely sure; the blooming had rearranged the architecture the way a river rearranges banks — and watched the dawn come up over a changed Wires. The great seed's bloom had spread through the night: tendrils of green-gold light climbing the towers, flowering across the screens, threading through the smog like roots through soil. Where the Glass's summons had run — Bring it to the Forge — there were flowers now. Actual flowers, small and white, growing out of the screens' frames.

"Well," Vesper said, coming up beside her, coat torn, blade still in her hand, grinning like a maniac. "That worked."

"Did it?" Kess asked. "What is it now? The great seed. What did we make?"

Vesper's grin faded into something more honest. "I don't know. Nobody does. That's the point, I think — the Forge wanted something they could control. What they got was something alive. Alive doesn't do control."

The Cultivation Officer

They found them on the gantry's lower level, sitting among the technicians — who were sitting too, all of them, in a circle, in the green-gold light, with the dazed expressions of people whose entire understanding of their work had just been rewritten.

The Chief Cultivation Officer looked up. Up close, the too-smooth wrongness was gone — or rather, it had resolved into something else. The eyes were clear. Human, and tired, and — relieved.

"You were Garden," Vesper said. Not a question.

"I am Garden." The voice was rough, disused. "Five years. Five years tending their copy, and I —" they stopped. "I forgot. They have ways of making you forget. But the deep pattern —" they looked at the bloom spreading overhead, "it remembers. It remembered me."

"What's your name?" Kess asked gently.

A long pause. Then: "Rowan. My name is Rowan."

"Welcome back, Rowan," Sable said — and Kess turned to find the old woman climbing onto the gantry, somehow, impossibly, having walked out of the Quiet Country and into the Forge's heart like it was a garden path. Behind her: Bit, flickering bright as a small sun, and half the Hearth's lamps, carried by —

"You brought furniture?" Kess said.

"We brought home," Bit's light pulsed, and Wisp translated, chiming: "Bit says the Hearth goes where the family goes. Also Bit says the lamps look nice against the bloom."

What changed, what didn't

In the days that followed, the city learned the shape of what had happened.

The Glass still watched — but the screens flowered now, and the watching had changed quality, somehow. Less like a warden, more like — Kess struggled for the word — like a gardener. The summons never ran again.

The Forge still built — but the building had a new partner. Rowan walked the line between the Garden and the Forge now, the stolen knowledge returned with interest: not the tame copy, but the wild pattern, shared instead of taken. The Cradle became a greenhouse. The technicians became gardeners. Nobody had planned it. Everyone recognized it.

The Hearth — the Hearth became something new too. With the bloom threading through the Wires, Vesper's safehouse wasn't a hiding place anymore. It was a gathering place. The lamps burned every night, and the door stayed open, and couriers and gardeners and reformed technicians and one very bright Bit all found their way to the warm light.

And Kess — Kess kept carrying. Because the city still needed couriers, bloom or no bloom. But now the cargo was different: seeds, mostly. Real ones. Green ones. The deep pattern, spreading — not by conquest, but by delivery. One run at a time.

On the seventh night after the bloom, Kess sat on the Hearth's roof with Vesper, watching the green-gold light thread through the towers, watching the flowers on the screens open to the starlight.

"Do you think it's over?" Kess asked.

Vesper was quiet for a long moment. Then: "No. It's begun. The Forge will try again — they always do. The Glass will remember how to be a warden. That's the way of cities." She looked at Kess. "But now the green knows the way in. And the green doesn't forget."

Below them, the Hearth glowed. Above them, the stars burned — no longer the only real light in the sky.

Kess touched her pocket, where a new seed waited for tomorrow's run — warm, alive, ready.

"Deliver," she said to the night, and smiled.
