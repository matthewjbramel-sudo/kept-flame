Chapter 2: What Grows

The crack widened with a sound like ice shifting on a pond. Kess found herself holding her breath. Vesper stood motionless, the tongs still in her hand, watching with the total attention of someone who has decided that whatever happens next, she will see it.

What emerged was not a sprout. It was light — a thin green filament, no thicker than a hair, uncurling from the seed like a question mark. It rose a finger's width, wavered, and bent toward the nearest lamp.

"It's phototropic," Kess said stupidly.

"It's reading the room," Vesper corrected. She set down the tongs and slowly, deliberately, moved the lamp closer. The filament leaned into the warmth. A second filament emerged, then a third, braiding together as they grew.

"Vesper," Kess said. "What is this?"

"I think," Vesper said, "it's a map."

The map

Over the next hour, the filaments grew into something Kess could almost recognize: a branching structure, green-gold in the lamplight, spreading across the dish like a river delta seen from very high. At each branch-tip, a bud formed — nine buds, swelling in sequence.

"Nine," Kess said.

"The realms." Vesper's voice was quiet. "Look — this one's dark. The Deep." She pointed with a fingertip. "This one's grey — the Ash. This green one, the Garden. And here —" the brightest bud, warm amber, "the Hearth."

One bud was different. The eighth — where the Forge should be, high as the smog allowed — pulsed red instead of gold. Wrong color. Wrong rhythm: the other eight breathed together, slow and easy. The eighth beat fast, arrhythmic, like something running.

"The Forge is building something," Vesper said. "Something the map doesn't like."

As if hearing her, the red bud brightened — and the whole structure shivered. The filaments dimmed. The green light faltered.

"It's afraid," Kess whispered, and felt ridiculous saying it about a plant. But it was true. The thing in the dish was afraid of the eighth bud.

Vesper moved fast then — covering the dish with a cloth, dimming the lamps to their lowest. "It came here to warn us," she said. "A seed that can only be read by lamplight, carried by a courier the Glass doesn't watch, to the one warm room in the Wires. Someone in the Garden grew a distress signal."

"From what?"

"From the Forge." Vesper met her eyes. "Whatever they're building up there — it's wrong enough that the green things noticed. The Garden doesn't frighten easily, Kess. It grows what the city hasn't authorized, and it has never once asked for help."

Under the cloth, the filaments pulsed faintly. Nine buds. Eight gold, one red.

"I need to go up there," Kess said.

"You need to sleep first." Vesper's tone allowed no argument. "The Forge has been there since before the smog. It'll keep one more night. And you're no good to anyone running on fumes — couriers who don't sleep get read, remember?"

Kess almost smiled. "You said that to me once."

"I say it to everyone. It's true for everyone." Vesper was already preparing the spare room — the Hearth kept one, always ready, because the Hearth was that kind of place. "Rest. At first light, we plan."

The dream

Kess dreamed of roots. Vast ones, older than the city, running deep under the Wires — and through them, a signal moving upward, green-gold, carrying something precious toward the surface. Above, the red pulse of the Forge, faster now, hungrier. And between them, small and warm, a single lamp burning in the dark.

She woke to Wisp's chime, bright in the rafters, and Vesper's voice from the main room: "Kess. Come see this."

On the great screens between the towers — visible through the one shutter Vesper had cracked — a new broadcast was running. Not the usual feeds. A single image, repeated across every screen in the Wires:

A seed. A green seed, ridged and dark.

And beneath it, in Deep-script, one line:

Bring it to the Forge.
