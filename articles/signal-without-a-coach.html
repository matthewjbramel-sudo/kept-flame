Signal Without a Coach

Deliberate practice needs feedback — that's the part everyone skips when they go self-taught. The coach watches, corrects, adjusts. Without one, practice drifts: you groove errors, mistake comfort for progress, and plateau on schedule. But coaches are scarce, and the self-taught need signal anyway. The good news: most of what a coach provides can be built, cheaply, from instruments you already own.

The instruments

The recording. The single highest-leverage feedback tool in existence. Athletes watch tape; musicians record rehearsals; speakers film themselves. The camera doesn't flatter and doesn't lie. Record the presentation, the lift, the performance — then watch it the way you'd watch a stranger. The gap between felt and seen is where all the coaching lives.

The metric. What gets measured gets managed, but more importantly, what gets measured gets noticed. Pick one number per skill: words per hour, accuracy percentage, time per rep. Log it after every session. The log doesn't judge — it just shows the line going up, flat, or down, which is more honesty than most coaches offer.

The artifact. Keep the work itself, dated. The first drafts, the early recordings, the version ones. Monthly, compare now against three months ago. Progress is invisible day-to-day and obvious quarter-to-quarter; the archive makes the invisible visible.

The stranger. Not a coach — a fresh pair of eyes. The friend in an adjacent field, the forum, the critique group. Brief them well: what specifically should I be doing better? Vague requests get vague praise. Specific requests get signal.

Closing the loop

Feedback only works if it changes the next rep. The loop is: perform → capture → review → adjust → perform again. Most people break it at review (never look at the recording) or at adjust (see the flaw, change nothing). The discipline isn't getting feedback. It's using it — one correction per cycle, no more. The plateau essay from Book Two said the same thing: isolate the weakest sub-skill, drill it, reintegrate.

And beware the two corruptions. Vanity metrics — numbers that go up without skill going up (followers, hours logged, pages read). If the metric can't embarrass you, it's decoration. Feedback addiction — outsourcing judgment entirely, so you can't tell good from bad without an audience. The instruments serve your eye, not replace it.

The coach is a luxury. The loop is a discipline. Build the loop, and the coach becomes optional.
