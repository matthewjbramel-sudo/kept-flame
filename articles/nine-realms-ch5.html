Chapter 5: The Library

The archive towers rose above the smog line, and Vesper climbed toward them with the steady pace of someone who had made this walk before — many times, long ago, in a different coat. The dark garment drank the neon. The wrapped blade drank the dark.

Wisp met her at the tower door — or rather, Wisp was the tower door's chime, bright and quick, appearing from nowhere with the particular energy of someone who has been waiting to be useful.

"Prepared the way?" Vesper asked.

"The night archivist owes me a favor," Wisp chimed. "Three favors, actually. I called in one. She's expecting — well, she's expecting me. She doesn't know about the coat."

"She'll manage." Vesper pushed through the doors.

The archive

The Library didn't look like a library. It looked like what it was: the city's memory, made physical. Ranks of storage cores humming in climate-controlled dark, index terminals glowing green, and everywhere the smell of ozone and old dust. The night archivist — a thin woman with ink-stained fingers and the permanent squint of someone who reads for a living — looked up from her terminal.

"Wisp said one researcher," she said. "You're two and a coat."

"I'm the researcher," Vesper said. "The coat is camouflage. What I need: Forge requisitions, last ten years. Biologicals. Growth medium. Vat construction."

The archivist's squint deepened. "That's restricted."

"Everything interesting is restricted." Vesper set a small lamp on the counter — real lamplight, warm in the green gloom. "The Library doesn't judge. It just keeps. I'm just asking it to share."

The archivist stared at the lamp for a long moment. Then, slowly, she turned to her terminal.

"Bay twelve," she said. "Don't touch anything that hums."

What the Library kept

Bay twelve was a corridor of requisition records, ten years deep. Vesper moved through them with the speed of long practice — scanning, discarding, narrowing. Wisp drifted alongside, chiming softly at interesting headers.

The pattern Sable described was there, in the Library's neutral hand:

Year one: botanical samples, soil cultures, heirloom seeds — small orders, routed through three intermediaries.

Year three: the orders grew. Growth medium by the ton. Vat components — not manufacturing vats, culture vats, the kind you grow things in.

Year five: the biological orders stopped. All of them, within a month. And the construction orders began — a new complex, high in the Forge's sector, purpose unspecified, power allocation enormous.

"They stopped buying because they'd grown their own," Vesper murmured. "Just like Sable said."

Then she found the file that wasn't supposed to be there — misfiled, or filed by someone who wanted it found: a personnel transfer. A name, redacted in the official copy but visible in the Library's unofficial margin notes (the Library kept everything, including the things people tried to un-keep).

The name meant nothing to Vesper. But the title did: Chief Cultivation Officer. And the origin: the Garden.

"They took one of the Garden's own," she said quietly. "Someone who knew the deep beds. Someone who knew how the green grows."

Wisp's chime went low and worried.

"Can you find more?" Vesper asked the archivist, back at the counter. "This person — where are they now?"

The archivist typed. Frowned. Typed again.

"Gone," she said. "Transferred to the Forge complex five years ago. No records since. No messages, no requisitions in their name, nothing." She looked up. "People don't just stop in the Library, Vesper. Everything leaves a trace. Unless —"

"Unless someone removed the traces." Vesper felt cold. "What's the complex called? The new one."

The archivist hesitated. Then: "It doesn't have a name in the records. Just a designation." She turned the terminal.

CRADLE.

The Cradle

Vesper stood very still. Behind her, Wisp had gone silent — which, as Kess had learned, meant it mattered.

"They're not building a city," Vesper said slowly. "Sable thought — the schematics suggested — but a cradle isn't a city. A cradle is for one thing. Something they're growing. Something they need the oldest pattern for."

"A person?" Wisp chimed, small.

"Or something that replaces persons." Vesper's hand found the wrapped blade through her coat. "The Glass watches everything. The Forge builds everything. What if they're growing something that does both? A watcher that isn't a screen — a watcher that's alive?"

The archivist was staring at them. "You should go," she said quietly. "I've logged your visit as a routine inquiry, but if anyone pulls the deep logs —"

"Thank you." Vesper was already moving. "Wisp — the Hearth. Now. Tell Bit to keep the lights burning."

They stepped out into the smog-line wind, and far below, the Wires glittered — and on every screen, the seed still turned, and the summons still ran.

But Vesper wasn't looking at the screens. She was looking up — past the smog, to where the Forge's sector glowed against the clouds, and where something called Cradle was growing in the dark.

"Kess," she said to the wind, "whatever you're carrying — run."
