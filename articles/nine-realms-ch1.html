Chapter 1: The Live One

The Hearth was warmer than Kess remembered, or maybe she'd just been cold longer than she thought. Vesper set tea in front of her — real tea, smelling of smoke and something green — and sat across the low table with the particular stillness of a person who has decided to wait as long as it takes.

"Drink," Vesper said. "Then talk."

Kess drank. The warmth spread through her hands first, then up her arms, and she realized she'd been clenching her jaw since the vendor's stall. She made herself unclench it.

"Three nights ago," she said. "The Garden levels. A woman found me at the usual drop — except it wasn't the usual woman."

Vesper's expression didn't change. "Describe her."

"Old. Not aged — old, like the Deep is old. Grey coverall, Garden dirt under her nails, and eyes that had seen the screens go up." Kess turned the cup in her hands. "She pressed the message into my palm and said, 'It has to go to the Hearth. Don't let the Glass read it.' Then she walked into the green and I swear the plants leaned toward her."

"The Garden grows what the city hasn't authorized," Vesper said quietly. "Including people, sometimes. Did she give a name?"

"She said names are how the Glass finds you." Kess reached into her coat and set the message on the table between them.

It pulsed. In the lamplight of the Hearth, away from the neon, it looked different — not a data chip, not a letter. It was a seed. A literal seed, the size of a thumbnail, ridged and dark, warm as a coal and beating like a heart.

Vesper stared at it for a long moment. Then she did something Kess had never seen: she reached out — and stopped, her hand hovering, as if the seed were hot.

"It's growing," Vesper said.

The reading

"Seeds don't beat," Kess said.

"This one does." Vesper stood and crossed to a cabinet Kess had never seen opened. From it she took a shallow ceramic dish, a pair of tongs, and a lens on a brass arm. She set the dish on the table, and with the tongs — carefully, the way you'd handle something venomous or holy — lifted the seed into it.

Under the lens, the ridges resolved into script. Not printed, not etched — grown, the way grain runs in wood. Kess leaned in. The characters were old, older than the Wires, older than the screens. She recognized the family — Deep-script, the first writing, from before the city had a name.

"Can you read it?" Kess asked.

"Parts." Vesper adjusted the lens. "'When the eye opens' — that's the Glass, the screens. 'The root remembers' — the Deep. And here —" she traced one line with a fingertip, not touching, "'the flame kept is the flame shared.'"

"That's a Hearth saying."

"It's older than the Hearth." Vesper sat back. "Kess. This seed is a message, yes. But it's also a test. Someone grew a letter that can only be read by lamplight — not neon, not screen-glow, lamplight — and sent it to the one room in the Wires that still burns it."

"So it's for you."

"It's for us." Vesper's eyes were bright. "The woman in the Garden didn't choose a courier. She chose a witness. You're here because whatever this is, someone wanted you to see it happen."

Outside, the rain kept falling. On the great screens, the flicker came again — longer this time. Deliberate. Like an eye, opening, looking.

The seed in the dish pulsed once, hard — and a hairline crack appeared along one ridge.

"It's hatching," Kess whispered.

Vesper was already moving. "Bit — the shutters. All of them. Now."

The square of light shot around the room, and the Hearth went dark except for the lamps — and the seed, which glowed faintly green through the crack, like something remembering how to be alive.
