Money, Examined

Fifty-one essays on philosophy, and money finally gets its own. Not because money is the point — but because unexamined money quietly runs the point. The relationship with money shapes the relationship with work, with time, with freedom, with fear. You can examine everything else and still be driven by a wallet you never looked at.

What money is

Money is stored freedom — nothing more, nothing less. Every dollar is a small claim on future choice: the choice to say no, to wait, to help, to rest. This reframe changes everything. Spending isn't consumption — it's converting freedom into experience. Saving isn't deprivation — it's moving freedom forward in time. And debt is borrowing freedom from your future self, at interest.

The examined question isn't "how much?" It's what is it for? Money without a purpose becomes either a god (never enough) or a guilt (never deserved). Money with a purpose — the campaign, the freedom number, the generosity fund — becomes a tool. Tools don't run lives. People run tools.

The examined money rules

Know your number. Not the dream number — the freedom number. What does enough look like, concretely? Annual spending, safety buffer, the invested base that covers it. Vague money goals produce vague money behavior. The five-year campaign from the 52-map works because the number is named.

Spend on what you actually value. Audit the last month: where did it go? The gap between spending and values is the unexamined tax. Most people spend heavily on things they barely value and starve the things they claim to love. The audit fixes this in one honest hour.

Let compounding work. The long game from Book Five applies to money first: time in, steady contributions, no panic. The math is boring and undefeated. Becoming boring (Essay 6) was always partly about this.

Hold it loosely. Book One, Essay 6: the open hand. Money clutched becomes anxiety; money hoarded becomes identity; money worshipped becomes a master. The examined relationship is stewardship — managing it well, for purposes beyond itself, without letting it manage you.

The Tuesday-morning version

One hour, one audit. Income, spending, the gap. The freedom number, written down. One change — the subscription that doesn't serve, the automatic transfer that does. Money, examined, becomes what it always was: a tool for the life you're building.

The examined life doesn't despise money. It just refuses to be run by anything unexamined — including this.
